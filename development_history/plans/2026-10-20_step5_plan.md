## Why this plan changed

- **The panel:** five independent reviewers each read the whole project from one angle:
  - the goals against the methods;
  - the statistics;
  - the validation;
  - the physics;
  - a deliberately hostile "Reviewer 2".

  A chair then checked their most important numbers and merged the findings.
- **Their verdict:** run as drafted, Step 5 would fail its own checks, and its results would not survive peer review.
  Every fix is cheap and needs no extra GloGEM runs. With the fixes, the project supports a sound regional paper.
- **The most serious finding (I checked it myself):** the error model I estimated in Step 3 is wrong in form. It
  describes how far the model may normally miss a reading, and which readings tend to miss together.
  - **The check:** if the error model is right, the actual misses, measured in units of the expected misses, average
    about 1. Ours average 3. The calibration would then be about three times too sure of itself.
  - **The cause:** a missing small error that each reading has on its own: its exact depth, short wiggles the monthly
    model cannot resolve.
  - **With that one term added:** the check gives 1.05, and the best f_ref moves from 0.52 to about 0.70.
  - **Why my simulation test missed it:** it simulated data from the same wrong form, so it could not catch the error.

## What the panel found, in plain words

1. **The error model** fails its own check (above). The form must be chosen by evidence, and checked, before anything
   is fitted.
2. **One site dominates.** The Monte Rosa summit saddles hold over a third of all readings and about 70 % of the
   misfit. My new rule "no site above 25 % of the misfit" would therefore fail whatever the result. The checks on single
   sites must be redefined and actually programmed.
3. **The calibration is only tested against itself.** Today it must beat our own starting values and our own older
   target. A referee will ask whether it beats simple rules that anyone could apply:
   - the temperature predicted from elevation alone;
   - from the mean air temperature;
   - from the nearest boreholes;
   - Suter's known elevation limit of cold firn.

   That comparison must be fixed in advance, including what we do if we lose.

   **Your point (21 Oct): the model is meant for unseen glaciers, so a nearest-borehole rule should lose.**
   - **That is exactly how the benchmark is set up.** Every rule predicts a massif it has not seen, from the other six.
     Its "nearest boreholes" are therefore in another massif, just as for an unseen glacier.
   - **A quick check on the existing runs** (no new runs; deep readings only, each massif predicted from the others):

     | rule | error, all | in the zone 3000–3800 m | above 3800 m |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | GloGEM (FRHS, one f_ref) | **2.42 °C** | 2.51 | **2.89** |

   - **In the zone it does not yet.** There a straight elevation line does as well as the model or better. That is the

   - **Simple rules cannot give what the model gives:** temperature profiles, polythermal structure, beds close to

1. **Orientation (sun and shade) as a process.** *Revised 21 Oct.*

   The investigation had four parts:
   - four investigators: literature, code, data, and the evidence in our boreholes;
   - one design;
   - two hostile critics: one on the physics, one on how a referee would attack it;
   - a revision that answers both critics.

   I checked its main numbers against its own tables.

   **a. How orientation would enter the model**
   - **Only the meltwater heat of the thermal model changes.** The mass balance, rain, snow and firn stay exactly as
     they are, and a gate proves that.
   - **Each band gets an orientation factor F.** It says how much more or less this slope melts than flat open ground
     in the same weather. The thermal melt becomes F × today's melt.
   - **F comes from a simple hourly energy balance:**
     - the sun on the real slope, with its horizon (the week-1 terrain calculations);
     - plus the heat from the air (Giesen & Oerlemans 2012);
     - computed over 1981–2020 weather.
   - **The energy balance is matched to our own melt.** On flat ground it melts exactly what our model already melts
     in that band, June to September. So F carries only the effect of slope and shade, not a new melt level.
     - Our model's melt itself agrees with measurements: Col du Dôme 116 mm in 2011 against 110–120 mm measured;
       Corvatsch 1.5–1.7 m against 1.5–2.1 m from GLAMOS.
     - The first design skipped this matching and made the effect about twice too strong. The physics critic caught it.
   - **F changes with each month's warmth.** In a warm summer a shaded slope catches up with a sunny one. So F is a
     small table from 3 °C colder to 3 °C warmer than normal:
     - shaded bands gain 5–11 % per degree;
     - sunny bands lose 2–6 %.

     So in the colder spin-up climate shaded slopes get even less meltwater heat, and since 1972 the warming lets them
     catch up.
   - **Size of the effect:**
     - sunny Lys bands get 18–28 % more meltwater heat;
     - shaded Taconnaz bands get 12–53 % less;
     - flat saddles change by under 10 %.
   - **No constant is taken from the boreholes.** All come from the literature or from our own melt. f_ref stays the
     only calibrated meltwater parameter.

```
   air temperature ──► today's melt M ──────────────────────────► mass balance (unchanged)
                            │
                            ▼
   sun on the real slope ─► F = melt on this slope ÷ melt on flat ground   (same weather; table by warmth)
                            │
                            ▼
                     thermal melt = F × M ──► × f_ref ──► heat in the firn and ice
```

### 1c. The orientation build: three parts per band (new 21 Oct, for your review)

**The idea in one picture.** Every 10 m band of every glacier is split into three parts. Each part gets its own
orientation factor F, from the sun on its own pixels. The model then runs three times per glacier, once per part.
The mass balance is the same in all three runs; only the thermal model's meltwater heat differs.

```
        one 10 m band of a glacier                 three runs per glacier            each borehole is scored
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────┐        against the run of the
  │ gentle (slope < 10°)       F_G ≈ 1  │  ───►  │ run G: every band uses F_G │        part it sits in:
  │ north-facing (270–90°)     F_N < 1  │  ───►  │ run N: every band uses F_N │   ───►  a north-facing hole
  │ south-facing (90–270°)     F_S > 1  │  ───►  │ run S: every band uses F_S │        reads run N, etc.
  └─────────────────────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────────┘
```

Ice that flows down a glacier stays within its part in this scheme (the north side feeds the north side). That
roughly matches a flowline down each side of a glacier.

**Steps.**
1. **Factors for every band and part (about 3 days).**
   - **Pixels:** slopes, aspects and terrain horizons come from the 25 m terrain model (EU-DEM) everywhere, inside
     each glacier's outline.
     - GloGEM's own grids are too coarse on six of the calibration glaciers (56–100 m, e.g. the Gorner complex),
       which flattens slopes and would inflate the gentle part.
     - Pixels are joined to GloGEM's bands by elevation, and split into G, N and S by slope and aspect.
   - **The melt each band must match:** GloGEM so far writes the thermal model's melt only at the borehole bands.
     - A small output addition (off by default, so all other output stays byte-identical) writes it for every band
       and month.
     - One FRHS run of the 23 glaciers produces it (about 25 min).
   - **The energy balance:** matched to that band's melt on flat ground, exactly as in Stage 1, so F only carries
     slope and shade. A new, faster version of the hourly sun calculation does many pixels at once.
   - **The factor:** F of a part = the mean melt of its pixels ÷ the melt of flat ground, for dT −4 to +6 °C. An empty
     part takes the band's mean and is flagged.
   - **A check of the new code:** fed the holes' own geometry, it must reproduce the 99 Stage-1 factors.
   - **Scope:** first the 23 calibration glaciers (under an hour of computing). Later all 4079 glaciers of the region,
     for the map (about 3–4 h of computing plus one region run of GloGEM).
2. **The run set-up (about 1 day).** Three folders of tables (G, N, S) per strength. The existing switch needs no
   change: each run simply reads its part's folder. Each part run gets its own run folder, so the outputs and the
   profile archives cannot overwrite each other.
3. **Merging the three runs (about 1 day).**
   - Each hole is assigned to its part by its own slope and aspect (week 1 geometry): G if under 10°, else N or S.
   - A new merge step takes the three runs' outputs and builds one output in the usual format. Each observation
     point takes its value from the run of its hole's part.
   - Everything downstream stays unchanged: the scorers, the Step 5 emulator and the checks.
   - This needs the pooling per hole and year everywhere, which Step 5 already uses.
4. **Checks before use (about 1 day).**
   - The mass balance is identical in the three part runs, byte for byte.
   - With every F = 1, each part run reproduces FRHS byte for byte.
   - The part factors at the borehole bands are compared with the factors of the holes themselves (Stage 1).
   - The merged result is scored like Stage 1 (overall, zone, above 3800 m, classes, frozen beds, sun/shade pairs)
     against FRHS and against Stage 1. This is reported, not a gate, since you adopted orientation.
   - A max-effort reviewer checks the factor code and the merge.
5. **Book chapter 12 and the decision log (about 1 day):** the three tests of today (orientation, burial, the
   re-check), your decision with its reasons, and the build, with figures.

**What it costs later.** Every Step 5 run becomes three runs: a batch of 40 design points takes about 4 h on 12
sessions instead of 1.3 h, and the region-wide map runs three times as long.

**What stays a limitation.** Snow, firn mask and burial stay per band (shared by the three parts); no radiative
warming of the surface; no reflected radiation from the terrain; the near-melting shaded bands may be called cold.

### 2. The first 40 runs

FRHS, the 23 glaciers, 12 runs in parallel on the free hosts. They compute while step 3 is written. Nothing is fitted
or looked at until step 3 is committed, so the rules cannot be bent towards a result.

### 3. Fix every rule that decides the result, before any fit (about 2 days)

1. **Choose the error model by evidence.**
   - **The candidates, listed in advance:**
     - today's form;
     - plus the small error per reading;
     - that error only in the top 15 m;
     - a depth link that differs above and below 15 m (above 15 m the data want about 1 m, below about 120 m);
     - an error that grows with the years between two visits;
     - a separate size for the ice-core saddles;
     - a version with more room for rare large misses.
   - **The winner** is the one that explains the residuals best for its number of terms. It must pass the check above
     (about 1) overall, for shallow and deep readings separately, and for each site.
   - **Its sizes are re-estimated** once at the first result, and inside each test fold.
2. **Sites and their checks.**
   - A site is defined by a written rule (holes within about 1 km and 150 m), with today's hand-made table as a
     sensitivity test.
   - **For each site we check:**
     - whether its misses are within what the error model expects;
     - whether its own offset is unusually large;
     - whether leaving it out moves the result by more than the result's own uncertainty.
   - A site that fails is reported and gets a run of the analysis without it. Only the last check can stop adoption.
3. **Can f_ref and f_ins be told apart?**
   - **Under the old forcing, on 7 glaciers (chapter 7), yes:** the depth-by-depth comparison told them apart, because
     f_ins acts in the shallow readings.
   - **The deep readings alone could not,** and allowing for the emulator's own uncertainty blurred them again.
   - **For FRHS on 23 glaciers it is not yet known,** so it is reported as a result, not used as a pass/fail check.
   - **Only if they turn out not separable:** f_ins stays at its starting value (1.0), f_ref is calibrated alone, and
     their trade-off is carried into the uncertainty.
4. **Which readings, and the variants beside the main one.**
   - **Main:** all depths, with the corrected error model.
   - **Beside it:**
     - deep readings only;
     - no site above about 20 % of the information;
     - only boreholes rated in equilibrium and published;
     - a wider starting range for f_ins.
   - **One written rule for leaving out very deep, very old ice everywhere,** instead of the Colle Gnifetti-only rule.
   - **Readings above 0 °C** are set to the melting point and flagged, not deleted.
5. **How site disagreement enters the map:** the uncertainty for a glacier without boreholes is the larger of two
   numbers (decision 3):
   - the calibration's own uncertainty;
   - how much the result moves when each massif is left out.
6. **The benchmark, fixed now: leave one massif out.**

```
     7 massifs:  Mont Blanc | Monte Rosa | Mischabel-Saas | Valais west | Bernese Alps | Bernina | Ortles
     each in turn:  fit the error model, f_ref and f_ins on the other six  ──►  predict the left-out massif
                    fit each simple rule on the same six                    ──►  predict it too  ──►  compare
```

   - **Scored:**
     - the error at depth, overall and in the zone;
     - regime classes counted by site, and which classes are confused with which;
     - frozen beds close to melting, counted apart from very cold ones;
     - whether the stated uncertainty ranges contain the readings as often as they claim.
   - **Compared with:**
     - the stage-2 grid choice (2.51 °C);
     - the model at its starting values;
     - the simple rules (elevation, air temperature, nearest boreholes, elevation thresholds, Suter's cold-firn limit).
       Each is fitted on the same six massifs, so its "nearest boreholes" lie in other massifs, as for an unseen
       glacier.
   - **Uncertainty of every comparison:** resampling whole sites.
   - **Adopt (decision 5):** if Step 5 is at worst 0.1 °C worse than the stage-2 grid choice (with 90 % confidence),
     overall and in the zone, and its uncertainty ranges are about right.
   - **If not:** v1.0 keeps the stage-2 values with a spread, and every table is published.
7. **The map's probabilities:** drawn from the calibrated parameters and the fitted error model together. It is stated
   whether a probability refers to the band average or to a random point in the band.
8. **All of step 3 is committed at once,** and the commit is recorded. Only then is anything fitted.

### 4. Fit and test (about 1 day; 5 h of runs)

1. **The fit on the first 40 runs,** then 20 more runs where the answer lies. The width of that region is set by how
   much the left-out massifs disagree.
2. **A real GloGEM run at each left-out massif's result** (7–10 runs), so the benchmark does not rest on the emulator
   alone.
3. **About 15–20 extra runs at the result:**
   - without stretching the columns (as the map will run), scored at the frozen beds;
   - FRH and H;
   - the past-balance correction at all 7 f_ref values, so its verdict is fair;
   - advection at half and 1.5 times;
   - a colder climate before 1942 for the spin-up;
   - one with the measured ice thickness at the bed holes.
4. **The error sizes re-estimated at the result;** one more round if they move outside their ranges.

### 5. Results and writing

- **The benchmark table exactly as fixed in step 3;**
- classes per site;
- frozen beds split by how close they are to melting;
- **the zone:** shown as the diagnosed limitation, with the perfect-firn figure;
- **book chapter 12;**
- **for the paper:**
  - the decision log, including where we deviated from our rules;
  - the range of glaciers the model may be applied to;
  - a self-contained methods description and a table of limitations.

## Glossary

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| FRHS | the central thermal variant: no T_off in the thermal inputs, ERA5's lapse rate, Herron & Langway meltwater, and the snow refreezing S |
| f_ref, f_ins | share of the meltwater that refreezes and heats; scale of the snow's insulation |
| error model | how far the model may normally miss a reading, and which readings tend to miss together (same band, same hole, same site) |
| emulator | a fast statistical stand-in for GloGEM, trained on the design runs, so the fit does not need thousands of real runs |
| stage-2 grid choice | f_ref picked on the grid of 7 values in stage 2, leaving one massif out (error 2.51 °C) |
| zone | 3000–3800 m, the firn–no-firn transition |
| floor | how close a band model can come to one borehole, about 1.7 °C |
| massif | a group of neighbouring glaciers (e.g. Mont Blanc, Monte Rosa); 7 have deep readings |
| orientation factor F | how much more or less a slope melts than flat open ground in the same weather; multiplies the thermal model's meltwater only |
| energy balance | melt computed from the heat the surface receives: sun (minus what the snow reflects) plus the heat from the air |
| today's melt | GloGEM's degree-day melt: computed from air temperature alone |
| shade error inside sites | the trend that, within one borehole site, shaded bands come out too warm and sunny bands too cold |
| quick estimate | a prediction from the existing runs without new model runs (each band read off at f_ref × F) |
| GLAMOS | Glacier Monitoring Switzerland, the Swiss glacier measurement network |
| T_off | the temperature correction that each glacier's mass-balance calibration applies to its climate input; kept out of the thermal model's inputs |
| rank correlation | how well two quantities rise and fall together in order (1 = always together, 0 = unrelated) |
| part (G, N, S) | the gentle (slope under 10°), north-facing and south-facing share of a 10 m band; each gets its own F and its own model run |
| burial | the snow added on top of the firn each year (m water equivalent per year), which carries the firn downward |
| EU-DEM | the European 25 m terrain model, used for slopes, aspects and terrain horizons |
