# Where we are and what is pending (overview, 19 Oct)

## The goal

- **The model:** a physically sound, calibrated and defensible thermal model in GloGEM.
- **What it forecasts:** the thermal regime of Alpine glaciers: cold, polythermal, temperate, and
  frozen beds.
- **Where it must be right:** in the firn–no-firn zone, 3000–3800 m.
- **The deliverable:** a map of probabilities, P(class) and P(frozen bed) per glacier, band and year,
  for the past and the projections.

## Where we stand

- **Forcing:** FRH is the central variant (no T_off, ERA5's lapse rate, Herron & Langway meltwater).
- **Mass balance:** the standard calibration.
  - The snowline recalibration failed twice (daily and monthly), so the snow cycle is a stated
    limitation.
  - The target-period fix is on main and GloGEMflow.
- **Firn:** the firn ledger's rules reproduce your firn model. Fed GloGEM's balance, it builds firn on
  small high glaciers where there is none (Hohsaas, Corvatsch).
- **Scoring:** we count sites, not bands (20 sites, 7 in the zone). On the development glaciers the
  model is already at the floor, about 1.7 °C.

## Running now

**Batch b19** (53 runs, done about 16:30) answers three questions:
1. Does the ledger help as it is?
2. Would it help if the firn lines were right (the perfect-firn test)?
3. Should the firn column follow the ledger (3b-ii)?

## Glossary

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| FRH, H | thermal forcing variants: FRH the central one (no T_off, ERA5 lapse rate, Herron & Langway); H the older one with T_off |
| firn ledger | per band, the annual firn layers from the mass balance; the meltwater reaches no deeper than they are thick |
| perfect-firn test | the ledger fed observed (GLAMOS) instead of modelled balances: would it help if the firn were right? |
| 3b-ii | the firn column's density follows the ledger (firn above its base, ice below) |
| floor | how close a band model can come to one borehole: about 1.5–1.9 °C |
| Step 5, v1.0 | the Bayesian calibration of the thermal parameters; the frozen first version |
| zone | 3000–3800 m, the firn–no-firn transition |
| leave-one-out (fold) | fit without one part of the data (a massif, a glacier system, a period), test on that part, repeat for every part |
| reliability | whether events forecast with probability p happen about p of the time |
